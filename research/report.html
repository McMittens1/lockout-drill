Why Lockout Drill exists

People find out their accounts depend on each other in circles only after the phone is gone. The fix everyone recommends is a "lost-phone drill" done by hand on paper. I looked for a tool that runs that drill for you, across every account, and found research prototypes but nothing a person can use. So I built one.

The problem

Your accounts unlock each other, until one piece goes missing

A typical setup looks safe on a normal day. The Gmail password lives in the password manager. The password manager's second factor lives in an authenticator app on the phone. The authenticator backs up to Gmail. The carrier texts its sign-in code to the phone number, so getting the number back needs the carrier. Lose the phone abroad and every one of those links fails at once. The big providers have no human support, so the lockout is often permanent. Heirs hit the same wall.

Evidence

A recurring, current problem

  • Sep 2026Ask HN: How to recover Google auth after phone stolen? 124 points, 131 comments. The poster was fully locked out; eight or more commenters told similar stories.
  • 2022Ask HN: If I get locked out of everything, please try to help me 659 points. A phone failure cascaded into near-total lockout.
  • 2025Ask HN: Google login has circular dependency People hit these loops and misread them.
  • 2024–26Google recovery loops and lost-number lockouts: 10 years of data lost, recovery email accepted, then refused, old number gone, locked out while abroad.
  • 2025–26Heirs blocked by phone-based 2FA: a family with the password list still couldn't get in, digital end-of-life plans, Bogleheads: how do heirs get 2FA access?
  • ICISSP 2024Study of 185 real Google and Apple setups 10 Google users and 17 Apple users would lose their account by losing a single phone.
  • CHI 2022"I'm Surprised So Much Is Connected" Participants' account graphs contained cycles; one was locked out of both email accounts for 30 days after losing a number.

What people do now

The drill is done by hand, if at all

  • An HN user runs "little tabletop simulations" of losing the phone, a hospital stay and death. Another drew the graph by hand.
  • Security guides tell readers to draw their recovery graph, find the loops and run a lost-phone drill, and offer no tool to do it.
  • People keep printed backup-code sheets and digital-estate templates: static documents with no analysis.

What's new

The model is published. A tool people can use is not.

The lockout-set math dates from 2019, so it isn't claimed as new. After searching GitHub, npm and PyPI, Product Hunt, AlternativeTo, browser extension stores, app stores, Hacker News launches, papers and consumer guides, I found no maintained, consumer-usable tool that runs named loss scenarios across all your accounts and explains the result. What Lockout Drill adds:

Named drillsPhone stolen, robbed abroad, house fire, SIM hijack, forgotten passwords, email hacked, and your death from your heir's side.
Three speeds, not yes/noMinutes, days, or only by support appeal, because that difference is the whole story when you're locked out.
The reason, spelled outThe exact chain of what blocks each account, and the shortest loop that traps it.
Fixes that are provenEach suggestion is applied to a copy of your setup and the drill is re-run. Only changes that help are shown.
Providers' real rules29 templates built from 46 services' own help pages.
Offline, one fileNo account, no server. The page's security policy blocks every network request. Optional passphrase encryption.

How it was chosen

Fifty pains in, one tool out

Nine researchers each mined a different corner of the web for things people complain about and patch together by hand: developer tools, AI tools, desktop, browser, documents, niche professions, life admin, creators and students, and "I wish there was" threads. A tenth hunted for angles they missed. Eight candidates survived to the kill check, where each one had two agents trying to prove it already existed.

Providers' real rules

Templates come from the help pages

Five agents read the sign-in and recovery pages of 46 services. A few of the facts that change drill results:

AppleAny signed-in Apple device plus its passcode can reset the Apple Account password. A recovery key or security keys switch off Account Recovery. Legacy Contacts don't get Keychain passwords.
GoogleA passkey skips 2-Step Verification. Without a backup method, recovery takes 3–5 business days with no phone support. New recovery info takes 7 days to count.
BitwardenCan't reset a master password or remove 2FA. Its recovery code still needs the master password.
1PasswordA new device needs the Secret Key. A recovery code doesn't turn two-factor off.
CarriersSign-in codes go to the number itself, and eSIM moves usually need the old phone. The fallback is a store visit with ID or a call with the account PIN.
DiscordSupport cannot remove 2FA. Without backup codes the account is gone.

Verification

Tested, then attacked

  • 54 automated tests cover the engine and its loops, every scenario, the heir view, fire-proof places, CSV import never keeping a password or 2FA secret, encryption and tampering, hostile input files, storage rules, and all 630 quick-start answer combinations.
  • In a real browser: fresh install to results, applying and undoing fixes, persistence across reloads, encrypted save and open, the lock screen, phone-width layout, light and dark themes, the printed plan, and two tabs kept in sync. The network log showed only the page itself.
  • Opened from disk in Microsoft Edge: the app renders, and encryption and storage work.
  • An independent review by four agents, each with a different lens, with a separate skeptic trying to refute every finding. 29 findings held up and all were fixed with regression tests. They included model accuracy (a Windows laptop counted as an Apple device), data safety ("Lock now" could drop an unsaved setup) and privacy (unencrypted storage readable by other local files).

Limits

What the drill can't promise

  • Big providers score recovery on risk: a familiar device, location and account age matter. No model can promise an outcome, so every result shows the route it assumed.
  • Templates reflect help pages as of September 2026. Providers change these flows, and every assumption is editable.
  • The drill is only as good as the setup you describe. A forgotten signed-in laptop, or backup codes you never printed, change the answer.
